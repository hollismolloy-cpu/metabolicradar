Protein foundations.

Before we optimize anything else — carbs, fats, timing, recovery — we need to know exactly how much protein your body requires to perform. This week you find that number and start building the habit of hitting it.

The science
🥩
Builds muscle
Provides amino acids for building and repairing lean tissue after every training session.
🔥
Burns fat
Stimulates glucagon — the hormone that signals your body to use stored fat for energy.
✦
Keeps you full
Activates satiety hormones in the gut and brain. You eat less, crave less, focus more.

Your protein requirement is tied to your lean body mass — the weight of your muscles, organs, and bones, not your fat tissue. Fat has almost no protein turnover. It does not need feeding.

Using total body weight overestimates the target for someone carrying more fat, and underestimates it for a leaner athlete. The lean mass calculation gives you the real number.

Example: A 200 lb athlete at 25% body fat has 150 lb of lean mass. Their target at 0.8g/lb is 120g — not 160g based on total weight.

Training with insufficient protein is like renovating a house while simultaneously demolishing parts of it. The harder you train, the more costly it is to undereat protein.

Your body can use roughly 30–40g per meal for muscle protein synthesis. Eating 150g at once does not produce five times the effect — the excess is oxidized. Spreading your target across 3–4 meals maximizes the anabolic signal.

Rule: Build every meal around a palm-sized complete protein source. Aim for 30–45g per meal. Breakfast counts as much as any other meal.

Complete proteins — containing all 9 essential amino acids — come from meat, fish, poultry, eggs, and dairy. Plant sources generally need combining to achieve completeness.

A tablespoon of peanut butter measured by volume versus weight can differ by 20%. Over a week, small errors compound into a significant gap between what you think you are eating and what you actually are.

Weigh raw, not cooked. Chicken breast loses 25–30% of its weight cooking. Log cooked weight and you are systematically underestimating protein by nearly a third.

This week's standard: Use a kitchen scale. Log every protein source in grams, raw weight. Precision on protein is non-negotiable this week — everything else can flex.
